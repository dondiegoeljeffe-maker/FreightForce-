The Culture Report | Big Wic — “Show Power”

Real Recognize Real

Big Wic · Review · November 22, 2026

Some records sound like an artist trying on a life. “Show Power” sounds like a man reporting from one.

Big Wic doesn’t have to pretend. Born in Philly and raised around Church Street South in New Haven, the neighborhood locals called “The Jungle,” he came up the long way. He moved mixtapes through New England for the late DJ Kay Slay and built a catalog the independent way, one record and one city at a time. That history isn’t background noise on “Show Power.” It’s the reason the record works.

What comes through is lived experience. Wic talks about power the way someone talks about it after he’s had to earn it, protect it and keep his head when other people lost theirs. People who’ve walked through similar circumstances will hear that right away. You can’t fake that, and listeners raised on real hip-hop can always tell.

That’s why the record connects. It isn’t built around flexing for the camera. It’s built around presence, about holding your ground and letting people feel it. Over Young Taylor’s production, it’s the kind of record the real ones can bop to without needing a translation.

The momentum is real too. “Show Power” has Connecticut’s Hot 93.7 behind it, following a “Cheesecake” run that took Wic’s visuals to MTV. For a New Haven veteran pushing Connecticut hip-hop forward independently, that matters.

The Culture Report verdict: “Show Power” is authentic hip-hop from someone who’s lived what he’s talking about. It isn’t a performance, it’s testimony. If you came up the way Wic did, you’ll recognize it. If you didn’t, this is your chance to understand it.
